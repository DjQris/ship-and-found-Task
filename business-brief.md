# Dwella — Business Brief

**Product:** Dwella (initial release: Dwella Lite)  
**Location:** Abuja, Federal Capital Territory, Nigeria  
**Status:** Working product hypothesis; not yet validated with estate managers or residents.

## The business

Dwella is a subscription-based software product for residential estates in Abuja. It helps estate administrators issue service charges, record payments, reconcile balances and make payment records accessible to residents. Estate associations or managers would pay a recurring software subscription. Residents would use accounts linked to their units to see information relevant to them.

The first release focuses on service-charge transparency rather than trying to manage every aspect of estate operations.

## Who uses it

**Residents:** Primarily adults aged approximately 25–65 who need to check their dues, payments and receipts. They are expected to use mobile phones, on mobile data that may be intermittent. Their comfort with digital services will vary.

**Estate administrators:** Adults approximately 25–60 responsible for maintaining household records, issuing charges, recording payments and responding to residents. They may use phones and laptops without accounting expertise.

These age ranges, device patterns and skill levels are assumptions to test with real users.

## The one thing that must work

**A resident must be able to see an accurate, understandable service-charge balance backed by recorded charges and payments.** If the balance cannot be trusted, the rest of the product has little value.

## Three questions the business repeatedly needs to answer

1. **How much do I owe, and what charges make up that amount?**
2. **Has my most recent payment been recorded, and where is my receipt?**
3. **Which months have I paid for, and which are still outstanding?**

These are proposed recurring questions, not yet confirmed by interviews.

## How the interface should feel

Dwella should feel **trustworthy, calm, clear and approachable**. Residents are checking money they have paid or may still owe; ambiguity can create disputes. The interface should prioritise readable figures, obvious payment states, simple wording, accessible contrast and mobile-first layouts over decoration.

The proposed brand primary is **#286B46** (green). Typography should combine confident, restrained headings with highly legible body text and numeric information. The design must communicate accountability without unnecessary complexity.

## Initial scope boundary

The first release will not include visitor access, security patrols, hardware integrations or full accounting. These can only be considered after validating demand and the core payment-record workflow.
